# Battlefield

Status: Draft · Last updated: 2026-11-18
Related: [economy.md](economy.md), [turn-structure.md](turn-structure.md), [open-questions.md](open-questions.md)

## Purpose

The battlefield is half of the game's puzzle. Its special hexes give players
reasons to spread out, fight over ground, and adapt their plan to the map in
front of them. Famous landmarks live here, as places worth fighting over,
rather than as cards.

## Layout

- The battlefield is a **16x16 hex grid**.
- Each player's **stronghold** sits in the middle of their side, 1 hex from
  the flat edge.
- A defending stronghold deals no damage to attackers. Stronghold HP and how
  attacks against it work are still open (OQ-06).

## Map rotation

- The game has several battlefield maps. Each match is played on one map,
  chosen at random.
- Each map has its own arrangement of terrain and special hexes, so no two
  maps play the same.
- Players can see the map before the match starts, so they can plan their
  strategy around it.

### Map design guidelines

- **Balanced, not necessarily mirrored.** Maps don't have to be symmetrical,
  but both players should have a fair chance.
- **Fight for the center.** The strongest special hexes, including most
  landmarks, sit in or near the center or at key choke points. Winning the
  center and holding it consistently should give a player enough resources
  to push across the map and besiege the opponent.
- **Off-center landmarks are allowed.** Some maps may place a unique landmark
  closer to one side, as long as the map stays balanced overall.

## Special hexes

Some hexes are **special hexes** that generate resources for whoever controls
them. They're the capture points described in [economy.md](economy.md).

- **Control:** control of special hexes is updated at the end of the
  Resolution phase. A player gains control of a special hex if one of their
  units is standing on it at that moment.
- **Holding:** control stays with a player until the end of a Resolution
  phase where an enemy unit is standing on the hex. No unit needs to stay on
  it.

There are two kinds of special hex:

| Kind | Examples | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic | Village, Town, Mine, Farm | Resources only |
| Landmark | The Great Pyramids of Giza | Resources, and may also grant passive or active abilities to its controller |

Most special hexes are generic. Landmarks are rare, and each one is unique.
Like cards, a landmark's ability should be grounded in what the place was or
what happened there.

## Card types

Landmarks are no longer a card type. The four card types are Leaders,
Equipment, Events, and Tactics.
